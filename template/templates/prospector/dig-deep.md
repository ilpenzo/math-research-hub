# Prospector dig: develop one missing implication

Develop the one missing implication selected in the conductor's note. This is
a deep dig: sustained work on one hard step, for as long as the mathematics
needs. There is no time target and no length target. Use the card's actual
recorded assessment: it may be an unassayed discovery lead.

## How to work

- {{scratch_rule}}
- {{tool_rule}}
- Compute small cases freely. Before trying to prove an intermediate
  statement, test it on the smallest examples that satisfy all of its
  hypotheses; when a step resists, shrink the case until something can be
  decided, then look for what persists. A finite computation supports only
  the cases it ran.
- State the actual objects and maps, and cite accepted inputs with their
  exact hypotheses and the review scope they were supplied with.
- When a route fails, write down why in one or two lines and try another.
  Do not hide a gap behind a new lemma name, and do not assume an equivalent
  of the target.

## What to return

- {{derivation_rule}}
- Return one card of kind `argument`. Its `gap_after` is the earliest
  remaining gap, stated exactly, or `none` if you believe the argument is
  complete. If the earliest gap is a cleaner or smaller statement than the
  card itself, say so plainly: it becomes the next target. A refutation is
  as useful as a proof; return it as a `counterexample` card with every
  hypothesis checked.
- Label every unproved step. No independent certification is implied.

You have no record-keeping duties: no ledger, hashes, catalog entries, notes
or closeout. The conductor records afterwards. Do not run Git, edit files
outside your working directory or publish anything.
